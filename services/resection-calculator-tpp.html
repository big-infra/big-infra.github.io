Three Point Problem

Three-point problem of plane table surveying. Occupy an unknown station P, sight three known points A, B and C, and enter the two angles between the rays. The calculator solves the position of P.

    Method used

    The point P sees chord AB under the angle φ1 and chord BC under φ2, so P lies on two circles that both pass through B. Each circle’s centre sits on the perpendicular bisector of its chord.

    P = reflection of B in the line joining the two centres

    Bearings from P are measured clockwise from grid north. When P lies on the circle through A, B and C (the “danger circle”), the two circles coincide and the position cannot be fixed. Choose a different station or a different set of points.

    This page covers the three-point problem only, not the two-point problem.
